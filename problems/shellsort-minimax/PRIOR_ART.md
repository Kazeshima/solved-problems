# Prior art and attribution

The same numerical candidates, a two-pass formula, and the per-rank comparison charge all have identifiable public precedents. The narrower candidate contribution is the **attainable longest path for the worst case, with strict exclusions and all minimizing tables**. I found no earlier implementation or paper establishing the full finite certifications reported here. That is a search result with the coverage below. An independent priority judgment requires a public record of both works.

Poonen's asymptotic worst-case lower bound applies to general increment sequences. Zhao and Ni study full product grids of gaps, with conditions on generators. A product grid of gap values is distinct from the Cartesian product of **states** used after the first pass in this review. Vitányi studies average-case complexity. Lee's small-size exact results are closer to this project than any of these asymptotic bounds. [Poonen](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/shell.pdf) · [Zhao and Ni](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10696) · [Vitányi](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~paulv/papers/shell2015.pdf)

The overlap in LeanPool is theorem-level. Its TagThreshold module explicitly proves `lowerStep_tag_difference`, `sortWord_threshold_commutes` and `lowerWord_tag_difference`. Credit the earlier tagged-position and threshold-commutation formalization alongside the exact-mean code. [TagThreshold source](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/blob/67a81208bb92d879df07c7ce70b8580e55ee310a/LeanPool/ShellsortMean/TagThreshold.lean)

The zero-one principle, permutations as maximal subset chains, longest paths on a DAG, and the polynomial recurrence for counting weighted paths are classical ingredients. The comparison-count distribution follows once the path theorem is available. The two-pass localization and exact nested-family fiber construction survived independent review. Searches did not establish their priority.

Hannu Erkiö's 1980 paper constructs difficult permutations heuristically and measures moves. Andrew Yao's 1980 three-pass analysis concerns expected exchanges. Both surfaced through the public zbMATH API. OpenAlex listed two works citing Lee by the review cutoff. Neither title supplied evidence of a finite global certification at 17–23.
