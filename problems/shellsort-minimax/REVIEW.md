## Derivation and implementation audit

**Execution.** A key at original chain offset $t$ sees its $t$ original predecessors, now sorted. With $s$ strictly smaller, it shifts $t-s$ larger keys. It compares once more exactly when $s>0$. Thus an empty prefix costs zero, a key smaller than every predecessor costs $t$, and a key larger than every member of a nonempty prefix costs one. These cases explain why a swap-only model and a sentinel implementation would give different answers. Switching the visitation order of disjoint residue chains preserves the full charge by rank.

**Thresholds.** With $S_k=\{i:A_i<k\}$, sorting a residue preserves its selected count and makes its selected slots a prefix. Therefore $T_h$ commutes with threshold projection, preserves cardinality and inclusion, and an added position $x$ moves to $(x\bmod h)+h|S\cap R_{x\bmod h}|$. Freshness supplies the otherwise necessary in-range condition. The Lean proof establishes the geometry of short last rows and relates the predecessor count to the integer quotient. Its counted loop visits each nonempty predecessor cell on the scan, returns immediately on a failed comparison and records the inserted key once per visit. The proof transports a marked rank through the actual sorted outputs of this loop.

**Optimization.** The unique position of each rank gives a maximal path. Each maximal path assigns each rank to one position and hence reconstructs a unique array. A path edge can recover its charge for every pass seeing only the adjacent thresholds. Conditioning on the first or last edge gives Bellman's recurrence and gives an attaining path. The Lean result for the arithmetic edge uses the same `earlierSmall`, `binaryPass` and `nextRankPosition` as the submitted model. It quantifies over every bijection of ranks and positions. The recursion used to state the theorem can revisit the same state. Tabulation by subset, as used in the solvers, gives the stated complexity.

**Compression.** For first gap $h$, fix the set of keys assigned to each residue. Its sorted output is determined. Reversing each chain at the input charges its full pair count and realizes that output. Since every output fiber has such a preimage, it is safe to add this maximum once. At threshold $k$ the digits $d_r$ count selected prefix slots. Their sum is $k$. A legal digit step chooses the next slot in one chain. Reading the steps reconstructs an entire sorted output, including every assignment of ranks to chains. Consequently the product-state recurrence is exact even for nondividing gap sequences. If $n=qh+r$, the product has $M=(q+2)^r(q+1)^{h-r}$ states and $\sum_i M m_i/(m_i+1)$ edges, with $m_i$ the chain lengths. First-pass reversal alone already appears in Lee. [Lee, section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.11127)

**Cost accounting.** There are $n2^{n-1}$ full-lattice edges. A scan of $n$ positions per pass at one source makes the subsequent per-edge work $O(p)$, giving $O(pn2^n)$ time and $O(2^n+pn)$ working words. Summing a loose upper bound over all legal tables gives $O(n^2 4^n)$. These bounds assume a word can hold an $n$-bit mask and a DP index. The supplied C++ implementation precomputes pass images for all compressed states and uses $O(pM)$ words in addition to the residue lookup tables and DP. The optimized Python verifier counts bits on Python integers. Its allocations and bit complexity differ. Reverse full-lattice tabulation in `potential.cpp` retains one two-byte remaining-cost value per subset. These statements describe concrete algorithms separately from the mathematical recurrence.

**Distribution.** Starting at polynomial 1, multiply along a step by the monomial for the edge cost and sum over alternatives. The bijection ensures every permutation contributes one monomial. The terminal degree is the worst count, the coefficient sum is $n!$, and the first two derivatives give the first two factorial moments. An edge at level $k$ has $k!(n-k-1)!$ completions. This also proves the direct mean formula. A maximum-first-pass compression changes the distribution. It could be extended by weighting the internal orderings of each chain with their own insertion-cost polynomials. The present distribution algorithm uses all subsets.

**Certificate logic.** Descending gaps plus the compulsory 1 give a bijection with the $2^{n-2}$ row masks. Every pass has at least $n-h$ comparisons. Every executable permutation witnesses a lower bound. A rejected row must be strictly above the candidate optimum. An optimum row requires an attained lower bound and an upper bound. Consecutive coverage, a nonempty winning set and a terminal record establish completeness. The seeded search throws if it encounters a smaller optimum while producing a fixed-header certificate. A partial file from that run cannot pass the checker.

## Repairs and adversarial tests

The supplied checker used Python assertions for soundness-critical checks and for work with side effects. Running Python with optimization removed comparisons and even winner-DP recomputation. Its record parser also ignored trailing fields on B, O, X and END. The repaired checker validates arities, IDs, widths, ties, row order, scope and termination through explicit control flow. Its default computes every winner upper bound. Exclusions-only output labels its scope. The review also fixed nonportable bit intrinsics, made the C++ command-line mode and integers explicit, and detects a failed final certificate write. The untouched archive and baseline commit preserve the supplied versions.

The initial independent-oracle run exercised its original `--small 7` test and all five complete files with assertions enabled. The hardened verifier was then run with `-O`. A separate copy-and-shift implementation checked all supplied maximizing witnesses and their pass costs. For every legal schedule through $n=8$, brute force agreed with the complete comparison histogram and the independent full DP. The size-eight part alone covers 2,580,480 table/input executions. Six hundred further seeded schedules through size 32 tested every rank's charge on every pass. Sixteen deliberately malformed certificates, including tied witness and baseline exclusions, extra records, missing coverage and incorrect winning costs, were rejected. ## Restricted gap families

For two passes $(h,1)$ write $n=qh+r$. The first pass contributes $h\binom q2+rq$. To bound inversions between two sorted chains, pair off-diagonal candidate inversions using their increasing orders. They cannot both occur. For equal lengths $m$, at most $\binom{m+1}2$ inversions remain. If the earlier chain has one extra slot, each extra candidate competes with a diagonal inversion, with the same bound for the shorter length. Summing gives $\binom h2\binom{q+1}2+(q+1)\binom r2$. In the first equal-length group, any nonrecord in the first row loses a diagonal inversion to an earlier chain. Choosing one such earlier chain for each nonrecord charges distinct pairs and pays for the extra failed comparisons. This accounting also bounds arrays that give up some inversions in exchange for extra failed comparisons. Optimizing the record count only on inversion maximizers would leave that trade-off open. The upper bound is reached by assigning smaller rank intervals to the long chains, descending intervals within each length group, then reversing input chains. The comparison correction is $n-(r\text{ if }r>0\text{ else }h)$. Dokken's July implementation gives the identical formula. [Source](https://github.com/mkdokken/ShellSort/blob/cc4a0533c6ce9cc4eaa4d371141621eba9bbcdf0/worst_case_two_gap.c)

Within a fixed quotient block with positive remainder, four times the cost is a convex quadratic. Testing the two integers bracketing its vertex after clamping, and testing the divisible endpoint separately, preserves all ties in $O(\sqrt n)$ arithmetic evaluations. For localization, center the first two excess terms at $T=\sqrt{2n}$. The two nonnegative penalties are $n(h-T)^2/(8h)$ and $q(2r-h)^2/8$. The error is $O(n)$ once $h=\Theta(\sqrt n)$. The latter follows for every minimizer from the nonnegative $qn$ and $qh^2/2$ terms of the first expansion in ROUND2 and the explicit upper bound. For that bound, put $A=\lceil\sqrt{n/2}\rceil$ and use the first $t$ with $2A^2+A+t-2t^2\le n$. The preceding step controls the overshoot by $4t-3$, and $t=O(n^{1/4})$. Then $h=2(A+t)$ has both centered defects $O(n^{1/4})$. Substitution gives $h=T+O(n^{1/4})$, $r/h=1/2+O(n^{-1/4})$, and minimum $n^2/4+n^{3/2}/(2\sqrt 2)+O(n)$. The argument is uniform over ties. The asymptotic proof is analytical.

For nested gaps $a>b$ with $b\mid a$, every $b$-chain is a local $a/b$-sorted array. Given **any** sorted output of this chain, its local rank order can realize a maximizing preimage for the second-pass term above. Independent choices in the disjoint chains preserve the whole output. Backward construction thus attains all stage bounds simultaneously, including when the first gap does not divide $n$. The divisor DAG has $O(n\log n)$ edges and computes exact nested optima. The baseline term in the overhead proof telescopes by pair counts. Ratios at least four give a linear-in-ratio overhead through equal-length subchain pairs, and bounded ratios below the possible initial exception give linear overhead from failed comparisons. Summing the logarithms forces $\Omega(n\log n)$ overhead. Powers of two attain the upper order. The nested optimum is $n(n-1)/4+\Theta(n\log n)$.

For power-of-two gaps at $n=2^m$, ordering ratios increasingly improves the intermediate-gap term. Splitting exponents at least four, merging two ones and replacing two threes leave the two parity cases in ROUND2. Direct comparison gives only ratios 4 at even $m$ and an initial 2 followed by 4s at odd $m$. All power-of-two subsequences were checked through $m=14$. The quotient-block method matched all minimizers for $3\le n\le420$, and witnesses and polynomial identities were checked at the stated ranges. At $n=5$, the nonnested table $(3,2,1)$ has stage maxima $(2,4,6)$ and joint maximum 11. Thus the fiber argument requires its divisibility hypothesis. [Restricted-family checks](logs/restricted.txt)
