**Checking the old graphs.** For a fixed vertex $u$, set $b(u)=\min_{S\ni u,\,|S|\le k}c(S)$. If a feasible vector has mass less than $b(u)$, its deficit can be added at $u$. Hence every Pareto point has mass at least $b(u)$. For both of DSM's graph6 strings, exact enumeration finds that the sets through vertex 0 attaining $b(0)$ cover every vertex. Consequently $b(0)e_0$ is Pareto-efficient and $\rho=b(0)$. This rules out a B counterexample on either old graph, including vectors different from DSM's displayed ones. The closest general theory found concerns bases of polymatroids. The equal-total-mass property of maximal vectors applies under polymatroid hypotheses. Here the small sets index the inequalities and the cut function decreases on some inclusions. More concretely, $h(G)\mathbf{1}$ is feasible and has total $40/3$, and some efficient point dominates it (maximize the total over its compact feasible upper set). The efficient vector exhibited here has mass 6. Thus the equal-base-mass property itself fails for this example. [Edmonds, *Submodular functions, matroids, and certain polyhedra*](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-36478-1_2)

Yoshida's Cheeger inequalities compare conductance to a normalized Laplacian eigenvalue. Work on computing edge expansion minimizes a cut ratio. The Mihail–Vazirani literature instead concerns the expansion of the graph of a 0/1-polytope. No bound on the least mass of the coordinatewise maximal points of this small-cut polytope was found in these results. [Yoshida](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08781) · [Computing edge expansion](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Expansion_mpc_final.pdf) · [0/1-polytope expansion](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~samotij/papers/polytopes_v4.pdf)
