## Mathematical verdict

**Verified on paper, with one local correction.** At line 123, the last claim about $f_\eta(y)$ requires $0\le y\le d$. Taking $y=-d$ and $\eta=+1$ gives $-\sin d\cos^N d-\cos d\sin^N d<0$. The later use at lines 165–168 takes $y=-x\in[0,d]$, so the corrected statement proves exactly what is needed. I found no counterexample to the conjecture or to the other inequalities on their stated domains.

For even $N\ge2$ define $d=\pi/[4(N+1)]$, $e=(-1)^{N/2+1}$ and $Q_N=2^{(N-1)/2}$. The maximum is

$$m_N^*=Q_N\,[\cos^{N+1}d+e\sin^{N+1}d].$$

The upper bound holds on the larger set of behaviors with $E(\mathbf0)=0$. The attaining behavior gives probability $2^{-N}$ to each output at that input. A classical mixture supported on just $0^N$ and $10^{N-1}$ with equal weights also has zero parity, so attainment needed a separate check. The conjecture asks about the joint output distribution. The paper's stronger statements about randomness against an adversary use its self-testing and decoupling results. [Paper, §4.3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.07030v3#S4.SS3.SSS1)

## Audit of the submitted argument

**§1, lines 9–27.** For a subset $S$ of settings equal to one, the argument of the cosine in Eq. (1) is $(N-1)\pi/4-|S|\pi/2$. Dividing its prefactor by $Q_N$ yields $2^{1-N}$. For $N=2$, Eq. (1) is CHSH divided by two. The expression $r$ is positive because $0<d<\pi/4$. Eq. (27) defines the target by evaluation at the angle in Eqs. (25)–(26). [Paper, Eqs. (1) and (24)–(27)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.07030v3)

**§2, lines 29–57.** Let $A_0=\prod_j A_j(0)$ and $U_j=A_j(0)A_j(1)$. Multiplication on the left by $A_j(0)$ cancels the first factor in $U_j$. All reorderings needed to combine different coordinates involve different parties. A product of two local reflections is unitary, and $U_j$ commutes with $U_k$ and $U_k^*$ for $k\ne j$. The unital C*-algebra that they generate is abelian. Each of its characters sends each $U_j$ to a number of modulus one. The isometry of the Gelfand transform therefore bounds $\|P(U)-\tau I\|$ by the torus supremum. Multiplication on the left by $A_0$ preserves the norm. A state has functional norm one, covering mixed states and purifications with Eve in arbitrary Hilbert dimension. Its expectation of the subtracted operator loses the shift exactly when $E(\mathbf 0)=0$. No choice of GHZ state or of common measurement angles enters this upper bound.

For the binary POVM extension, each contraction $X$ commutes with $D=(I-X^2)^{1/2}$. Thus the block matrix on line 51 is self-adjoint and squares to $I$. Both settings use the same local inclusion $V$. The corresponding two spectral effects compress to $(I\pm X)/2$. Tensoring the inclusions consequently preserves each joint Born probability. The paper itself uses local tensor-product projective measurements in §2.1. The draft limits its contraction extension to the tensor model, which suffices. [Paper, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.07030v3#S2.SS1)

**§3, lines 59–65.** The primary maximizing set is nonempty and closed in a compact torus. The secondary continuous objective attains a maximum there. Write two distinct phases as $u e^{iv},u e^{-iv}$, choosing half the sum and half the difference of arguments. Their midpoint parameter $w=\cos v$ belongs to $(-1,1)$, including $w=0$ for opposite phases. In the two-variable section $A+B(z_i+z_j)+Cz_i z_j$, the squared norm restricted to the chord has quadratic coefficient $4|B|^2$. If this is positive, at least one endpoint strictly improves the primary value. If it vanishes, both endpoints stay in the primary maximizing set and the secondary squared norm has coefficient $4$. This proves existence of a diagonal maximizer and covers constant polynomials. For example $F=z_1z_2$ has off-diagonal maximizers too. The same maximum identity follows from Grace–Walsh–Szegő for the closed unit disk followed by the one-variable maximum modulus theorem. [Borcea, Brändén and Liggett, Theorem 4.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2340)

**§4, lines 67–96.** Expanding $2\cos(\phi-k\pi/2)$ into the two complex exponentials gives exactly the $2^{-N}$ binomial formula. On the upper semicircle $1-iz=2e^{ix}\cos x$ and $1+iz=-2ie^{ix}\sin x$. The relative phase is $e^{-2i\phi}(-i)^N=e^{i\pi/2-iN\pi}=i$ for even $N$. Coefficients of $W$ are real. Conjugation supplies the full lower semicircle and preserves distance to a real center. In residue order $0,2,4,6$, the four functions corresponding to $C(x)$ are $\overline{C(x)},C(x),-\overline{C(x)},-C(x)$, evaluated at upper parameters $-x,x,-x,x$. Their centers are $t,t,-t,-t$. The extra conjugation of the actual phase in residues 0 and 6 gives the positive imaginary contact. At $x=d$, the rotation angle is $\pi/2-d$, so the real and imaginary components are exactly $t,r$. Reversing the sign of a nonzero center fails already at this contact.

**§5.1, lines 102–123.** The inequality $N/(N+1)^2\le4/25$ reduces to $(4N-1)(N-4)\ge0$. Substitution of $3<\pi<22/7$ gives all four bounds in (3). For $0\le y\le1/2$, integrate $\cos y\le1$ and then $\sin y\le y$ to obtain the quadratic lower cosine bound. A further integration gives the cubic lower sine bound. The logarithmic derivative of cosine proves the Gaussian upper bound. Bernoulli is used only where the base lower bound is nonnegative. At $d$, the lower multiplier is $(215/216)(19/20)-1/216>9/10$ and the upper one is at most $1+1/216<101/100$. The missing lower endpoint in the last sentence is corrected above.

**§5.2, lines 125–137.** Direct differentiation gives the two terms at line 129, including the minus sign on the $\eta$ term. On $[0,2d]$ their lower estimate is $(4/5)(1/2)-4/27>0$. On $[0,d]$ it is $(19/20)(7/8)-4/216>3/4$. The factor $N-(N+1)\sin^2y$ is nonnegative on both intervals. The sequences $j/b^{j-1}$ decrease for $j\ge4$ for $b=3,6$, by the ratio $(j+1)/(bj)$. Integration from $x$ to $d$ has the direction used at line 137. In particular $g(d)>(9/10)d>(3/4)t$ for the opposite sign.

**§5.3, lines 139–150.** The factor $1-(N+1)\sin^2 y$ in $h'$ decreases on $[0,\pi/4]$. Hence the minimum is at one of the endpoints even if the factor has no zero in the interval. The two endpoint multipliers relative to $d$ are bounded below by $2(53/54)(4/5)$ and $3(23/24)(11/20)$, both strictly greater than $3/2$. Since $\tan y\le4/7<3/5$ and $N-1\ge3$, both choices of $\eta$ have $f_\eta(y)>(147/125)d>t$.

**§5.4, lines 152–172.** Write $b=\pi/4+Nx$ and $\alpha=b+x$. The cosine-power part of $C'$ is $iN e^{i\alpha}\cos^{N-1}x$, and the sine-power part is $iN e^{i\alpha}e\sin^{N-1}x$. Expanding $2\operatorname{Re}(C'\overline{C-t})$ gives exactly (6), including $+t\sin\alpha$. Since $\cos x\ge|\sin x|$, $R\ge0$ up to both endpoints. On the negative half the bracket is $f_{-e}(-x)+t\sin\alpha$. In the three negative intervals the applicable sine lower bounds are $-1,-\sqrt2/2,0$. On $[0,d]$, the chord lower bound for sine gives a margin of at least $(3/4-t/(2d))(d-x)$. On $[d,2d]$ and $[2d,3d]$, $f_e\ge t$ and $\sin\alpha\le1$. The closed intervals share their endpoints and the derivative may vanish at the contact. Non-strict monotonicity proves the claimed maximum.

**§5.5, lines 174–198.** The derivative of $\cos^{2N}y+\sin^{2N}y$ has the indicated sign throughout $[0,\pi/4]$. At $3d$, $a=9Nd^2/2<9/20$, so $1-a+a^2/2\le1-(279/80)Nd^2<1-(13/4)Nd^2$. Also $81d^4<(3/8)d$. The radius lower bound uses $\pi/8+1/1296<41/100$. Finally $(13/4)(3/5)-3/8-101/100=113/200>41/100$. Both signs of $x$ are covered by symmetry of the modulus. None of these estimates is asymptotic.

**§6, lines 200–217.** In the standard Pauli convention the two flips on $|0^N\rangle,|1^N\rangle$ have phases $e^{ik\theta}$ and $e^{-ik\theta}$. With relative state phase $+i$ this gives $+\sin(k\theta)$. On the all-$X$ measurement, the amplitude has squared modulus $2^{-(N+1)}|1+i(-1)^{|a|}|^2=2^{-N}$ for every output. Substituting the four angles into $(N+1)\theta/(2\pi)$ gives $3N/4+1,N/4+1/2,N/4,3N/4+1/2$ in residue order $0,2,4,6$, exactly Eq. (26). Thus the contact gives the published value in Eq. (27).

**Independent two-party proof.** In the realification of a purification let $a_i,b_j$ be the four observable-applied vectors. They have norms at most one and $E_{ij}=\langle a_i,b_j\rangle_{\mathbb R}$. Set $u=b_0/2+b_1$, $v=b_0-b_1$. Since $E_{00}=0$, twice the normalized CHSH value is at most $\|u\|+\|v\|$. Expanding squares gives $2\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2=(3/2)\|b_0\|^2+3\|b_1\|^2\le9/2$. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz yields $\|u\|+\|v\|\le3\sqrt3/2$. The matching angle is $2\pi/3$, hence $m_2^*=3\sqrt3/4$. [Wooltorton, Brown and Colbeck, bipartite result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00124)

**§7, lines 219–223.** The description of the submitted Lean source is accurate about its logical scope. Defining the two disk predicates, and proving a finite-mixture implication with a disk premise, leaves both the scalar extremum and the general quantum upper bound to establish. The compiler findings and additions are recorded separately in FORMALIZATION_STATUS.md.
