# Scientific assessment

**Assessment dated 25 November 2026.** The classification merits a standalone mathematical paper. The earlier note supplied one family of counterexamples. The new result gives necessary and sufficient conditions for all integer $q\ge2,d\ge1$ and all real $0\le\beta\le1$. Its strongest contributions are the high-degree obstruction at positive temperature and the positive result for three colours. Their proofs use different properties of the same map.

| Component | Contribution and dependence |
|---|---|
| Positive temperature, $q\ge4$ | The active three-colour triangle survives adding arbitrarily many pinned occurrences to one active colour. A shifted supporting functional exposes it in the normalized target. The Cartesian support of independent inputs prevents realization of its relative interior witness. This covers fixed $q$ and arbitrarily large $d$, beyond the previous $q\ge d+2$ note. The extremal-coordinate method deserves explicit credit to Liu, Mani and Pernice. |
| Positive temperature, $q=3$ | The deterministic hull has exactly $3d$ edges. Every interior target can be reconstructed by bracketing its height at a fixed ratio and joining the brackets in the same ratio fibre. This explains why the four-colour phenomenon has a sharp threshold, supplies an algorithm for finding messages, and reduces the formal proof to real-variable continuity and the one-dimensional IVT. |
| Proper-colouring endpoint | The explicit star input realizes every simplex point when $d\ge q-1$, even for sparse targets. A positive normalizer is certified constructively. Together with the pre-existing sparse-degree negative result this completely describes convexity at zero temperature. |
| Elementary cases and prior work | The uniform map, the one-row affine map and the binary interval are elementary. The binary observation appears in the source paper. The zero-temperature sparse result and part of the positive-temperature family were already public in the earlier note. These components should not be presented as new standalone theorems. |

## Exact consequence for Question 4

Intersect the theorem with the printed interval $\max(0,1-q/(d+1))\le\beta\le1$, $q\ge3$, $d\ge2$. For every $q=3$ the answer is affirmative. For $q\ge4$ it is negative at every permitted $0<\beta<1$ and affirmative at $\beta=1$. At the permitted endpoint $\beta=0$, one has $d\le q-1$. The answer is negative for $d\le q-2$ and affirmative with full image at $d=q-1$. The independent classification also covers parameters outside that interval. [Liu–Mani–Pernice, Question 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06541v2#page=12)

The paper's Lemma A.1 identifies arbitrary-joint-law outputs with the convex hull of product outputs. In the nonconvex cases some joint inputs thus cannot be represented by a single product update. In the convex cases such a representation exists separately for each joint input, wherever the normalization is positive. Universality of the external fields and the contraction inequalities in graph-to-tree reductions are further requirements. Theorem 1.4 has an occupancy-tail hypothesis even when $d\ge2q$. The new result reaches this degree range while allowing all inputs. It gives no mixing-time, correlation-decay or approximate-counting bound on graphs. [Original definitions and bulk theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06541v2#page=5)

## Temperature and boundaries

At fixed $q\ge4$ and $d\ge q-1$ the image is nonconvex for every $0<\beta<1$, whereas the admissible image at $\beta=0$ is the full simplex. For every target the zero-temperature construction has nonvanishing denominator, so continuity and a finite net show Hausdorff convergence of the positive-temperature images to the simplex. At $\beta=1$ all factors equal 1 and the image collapses to the uniform point. These are changes in one-step image geometry. Gibbs phase coexistence and uniqueness on an infinite tree refer instead to limiting distributions under boundary conditions. A recent preprint reports a sharp uniqueness threshold, including a zero-temperature exception at $q=d+1$; its convex potential measures decay under iteration. [Wu–Xu–Yang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32635v1)

Throughout, neighbours may have different marginal laws and all entries may be real. At $\beta>0$ an omitted point has a neighbourhood outside the compact closed-domain image; perturbing the finite collection of witness inputs gives strictly positive witnesses to nonconvexity as well. The main classification concerns the closed domain. It does not prescribe a quantitative uniform lower bound on the probabilities. The Hausdorff and perturbation remarks are analytic corollaries outside the stated kernel targets.

## Priority and publication fit

No earlier equivalent full classification was identified in the bounded public search. Priority is **inconclusive**, including for the three-colour and zero-temperature constructions separately. A submission should acknowledge the supplied working draft's functional and winding construction, the prior note, the original paper's exposed-face strategy, and the role of AI assistance. The ratio-fibre interpolation is a short alternative proof developed in this review. [Prior-art audit](PRIOR_ART_AUDIT.md)

**Combinatorics, Probability and Computing** is a plausible first venue. The original problem connects combinatorial probability with approximate counting, and the result cleanly delimits one geometric mechanism. **Electronic Communications in Probability** is another plausible venue for a concise proof emphasizing the product-support obstruction and the ternary reconstruction. The subject fit is a judgment, not a prediction of acceptance. The four-page manuscript is a local research draft.
