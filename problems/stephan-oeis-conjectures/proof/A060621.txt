A060621: the number of flips between tilings of the cyclic zonotope Z(d+3, d)

A proof that  a(d) = (d+3)(d+8) 2^(d-1) = (d^2 + 11 d + 24) 2^(d-1).

26 November 2026.

Provenance.  The proof was found and written by Claude, an AI model made by
Anthropic.  Sections 2-5 were then formalised in Lean 4 (see Section 6).
The text has not been peer reviewed.


0. STATEMENT

OEIS A060621 (M. Latapy, 2001) lists the number a(d) of flips between the
d-dimensional tilings of the zonotope Z(d+3, d), i.e. the number of edges of
the flip graph in codimension 3:

    12, 36, 100, 264, 672, 1664, 4032, 9600, 22528, 52224     (d = 0..9).

R. Stephan observed in 2004 that these ten terms satisfy
a(d) = 2^d (d^2 + 11 d + 24) / 2 and listed this as Conjecture 69 in [S];
C. Barker (2013) gave the matching empirical generating function.

THEOREM.  For every d >= 0,   a(d) = (d+3)(d+8) 2^(d-1).

Equivalently, writing n = d + 3: the Hasse diagram of the higher Bruhat
order B(n, n-3) has n (n+5) 2^(n-4) edges.

Section 1 reduces the statement to a count of graphs, using known results.
Sections 2-5 prove that count; they are elementary and self-contained.


1. THE MODEL

Throughout n = d + 3 and [n] = {1, ..., n}.

For a (k+2)-subset X of [n], the packet P(X) consists of the k+2 sets X - x
(x in X), listed in lexicographic order; so X - x precedes X - y exactly
when x > y.  A family U of (k+1)-subsets of [n] is CONSISTENT if for every
(k+2)-subset X the members of P(X) that belong to U form a prefix or a
suffix of P(X).  (Consistent families are the signotopes of rank k+1 on n
elements of Felsner and Weil [FW]; compare the comment in A060602.)

We use two known facts about the higher Bruhat orders B(n,k) of Manin and
Schechtman [MS].

(F1)  The flip graph of the fine zonotopal tilings of the cyclic zonotope
      Z(n,d) is the Hasse diagram of B(n,d).
      [Z, Theorem 4.1]; stated in this form in [BW, Theorem 2.5].

(F2)  For 1 <= k < n, B(n,k) is isomorphic to the set of consistent families
      of (k+1)-subsets of [n] ordered by single-step inclusion, whose
      covering relations are the pairs U < U + {Y} of consistent families.
      [Z, Theorem 4.1]; stated in this form in [C, Definitions 2.1, 2.10 and
      Theorem 2.12].

Here Z(n,d) is taken to be the cyclic zonotope, as in the identification of
A060602 (the number of these tilings) with [Z, Proposition 7.1] recorded in
that entry.  For d = 0 the same description gives the Boolean lattice of
subsets of [3], with 8 elements and 12 covering pairs, in agreement with
A060602(0) = 8 and a(0) = 12.

Hence a(d) is the number of pairs (U, Y) where U and U + {Y} are consistent
families of (n-2)-subsets of [n] and Y is not in U.

Now replace every (n-2)-subset by its complement, a 2-subset {p,q}, i.e. an
edge on the vertex set [n].  A family U becomes a graph G on [n].  The
(n-1)-subsets are the sets X = [n] - p, and P(X) consists of the sets
[n] - {p,q}, q != p, in decreasing order of q; they correspond to the edges
at p.  Consistency therefore reads as follows.

DEFINITION.  Let S be a finite set of integers.  A graph G on S is
CONSISTENT if for every vertex p the neighbourhood N(p) is an initial
segment or a final segment of S - p (in the natural order).  A FLIP on S is
a pair (G, e), where e is a non-edge of G and both G and G + e are
consistent.

So a(d) is the number of flips on [d+3], and the Theorem follows from:

THEOREM 2.  For n >= 3 the number of flips on [n] is n (n+5) 2^(n-4).

Checks of the reduction.  Enumerating consistent families directly from the
packet definition for n <= 7 gives 8, 24, 62, 148, 338 families (A060602)
and 12, 36, 100, 264, 672 covering pairs (A060621).  Enumerating consistent
graphs for n <= 14 gives 2^n + n 2^(n-2) - 2n graphs, which is the value of
|B(n, n-3)| in [Z, Proposition 7.1], and reproduces all ten terms of A060621.


2. FLIPS AT A FIXED PAIR

Let S be a finite set of integers and G a graph on S.  The ROW of a vertex p
is the 0/1 word (r(q) : q in S - p), q increasing, with r(q) = 1 iff p ~ q.
G is consistent iff every row is monotone (non-increasing or
non-decreasing).  We use repeatedly:

(M)  If a monotone 0/1 word has a 1 somewhere before a 0, it is
     non-increasing: every entry before that 1 is 1 and every entry after
     that 0 is 0.  If it has a 0 somewhere before a 1, it is non-decreasing:
     every entry before that 0 is 0 and every entry after that 1 is 1.

Deleting a vertex from a consistent graph leaves a consistent graph, and so
does passing to the complementary graph.

Fix i < j in S and put

    A = {q in S : q < i},  B = {q in S : i < q < j},  C = {q in S : q > j},

with alpha = |A|, beta = |B|, gamma = |C|.  Unions below are written with +.

LEMMA 1.  Let G be consistent with i not adjacent to j.  Then G + ij is
consistent if and only if

    N(i) = A + B  or  N(i) = C,     and     N(j) = A  or  N(j) = B + C.

Proof.  Only the rows of i and j change.  Write the row of i in G as u 0 v,
where the displayed 0 is the entry at j, u is indexed by A + B and v by C;
the row of i in G + ij is u 1 v.  Both must be monotone.
 - If u contains a 1, then by (M) applied to u 0 v we get v = 0...0 and
   u = 1..1 0..0; a 0 in u would give the pattern 1, 0, 1 in u 1 v.  So
   u = 1...1 and N(i) = A + B.
 - If u contains no 1 but v does, then by (M) v = 0..0 1..1, and a 0 in v
   would give 1, 0, 1 in u 1 v.  So v = 1...1 and N(i) = C.
 - If neither contains a 1, then u 1 v = 0..0 1 0..0 is monotone only if u
   or v is empty, i.e. N(i) = {} equals A + B or equals C.
Conversely, if N(i) is A + B or C then u 1 v is 1..1 1 0..0 or 0..0 1 1..1.
The argument for j is the same, with the entry at i separating A from
B + C.  []

Accordingly define four classes of consistent graphs on S:

    DD(S;i,j):  N(i) = A + B,  N(j) = A;
    DU(S;i,j):  N(i) = A + B,  N(j) = B + C;
    UD(S;i,j):  N(i) = C,      N(j) = A;
    UU(S;i,j):  N(i) = C,      N(j) = B + C.

In each of them i and j are non-adjacent.  If |S| >= 3 the four classes are
pairwise disjoint (A + B = C or A = B + C would force S = {i,j}), and by
Lemma 1 the flips (G, ij) are exactly the members G of their union.

LEMMA 2 (symmetries).
(a) G -> complement of G + ij is a bijection DD(S;i,j) -> UU(S;i,j) and
    DU(S;i,j) -> UD(S;i,j).
(b) Let -S = {-q : q in S}.  The reversal q -> -q is a bijection
    DU(S;i,j) -> DU(-S;-j,-i) and DD(S;i,j) -> UU(-S;-j,-i); it exchanges
    the roles of A and C.

Proof.  Both operations preserve consistency, and (a) is an involution on
the set of flips at {i,j}, since the complement of (complement of G + ij)
+ ij is G.  The neighbourhoods of i and j are computed directly: for
instance in (a), if N(i) = A + B then i is adjacent in the new graph to
exactly the vertices of S - i - j outside A + B, that is to C.  []

COROLLARY.  If |S| >= 3, the number of flips (G, ij) on S is
2 |DD(S;i,j)| + 2 |DU(S;i,j)|.  Moreover |DU| is unchanged when A and C are
exchanged by reversal, and so is |DD|, because by (b) and then (a)
|DD(S;i,j)| = |UU(-S;-j,-i)| = |DD(-S;-j,-i)|.

Two lemmas organise the inductions.

LEMMA 3 (deleting a forced vertex).  Let F be a set of consistent graphs on
S, let w be in S, N a subset of S - w, and F' a set of consistent graphs on
S - w.  Assume
 (i)  every G in F has N(w) = N and G - w in F';
 (ii) for every H in F', the graph H + (w;N) obtained from H by adding the
      vertex w with neighbourhood N lies in F.
Then |F| = |F'|.

Proof.  G -> G - w and H -> H + (w;N) are mutually inverse.  []

LEMMA 4 (twins).  Let H be a graph on S - w and let t be a vertex of S - w
such that no element of S lies strictly between w and t.  Let G be obtained
from H by adding w.  If p is a vertex other than w and t, the row of p in H
is monotone, and p ~ w in G iff p ~ t, then the row of p in G is monotone.

Proof.  The row of p in G is its row in H with the entry at t repeated in
an adjacent position.  []

We say that w is a TWIN of t (t = i or j) if w and t are consecutive in S
and every vertex p outside {i, j, w} is adjacent to w iff it is adjacent
to t.  When a vertex w with a prescribed neighbourhood N is added to a graph
H of one of the classes on S - w, condition (ii) of Lemma 3 is checked in
the same way each time: the rows of i, j and w in H + (w;N) are explicit
words, monotone by inspection, and the rows of all other vertices are
monotone by Lemma 4 if w is a twin of i or of j.  Note that for p outside
{i,j}, in the class DU we have p ~ i iff p < j, and p ~ j iff p > i; in the
class DD we have p ~ i iff p < j, and p ~ j iff p < i.


3. THE CLASS DU

PROPOSITION 1.  |DU(S;i,j)| = binomial(alpha + beta + gamma, beta).

Proof.  Induction on |S|.

Case alpha = gamma = 0.  Every b in B is adjacent to i = min S and to
j = max S, so its row begins and ends with 1 and consists of 1's.  Hence G
is the complete graph minus the edge ij, which is consistent: the class has
exactly one element, and binomial(beta, beta) = 1.

Otherwise, by Lemma 2(b) (the binomial is symmetric in alpha and gamma) we
may assume alpha >= 1.  Let a = max A; then a and i are consecutive.  Let G
be in DU.  Since a ~ i and a is not adjacent to j, (M) shows that the row
of a is non-increasing: N(a) = (A - a) + {i} + B', with B' an initial
segment of B.

(1) Graphs with B' = B.  Then N(a) = {q in S : q < j} - a.  Deleting a
    gives a graph of DU(S - a; i, j).  Conversely, adding a with this
    neighbourhood to H in DU(S - a; i, j) gives a graph of DU(S;i,j) of the
    present kind: a is a twin of i, because p ~ a iff p < j iff p ~ i.  By
    Lemma 3 there are |DU(S - a; i, j)| such graphs, with parameters
    (alpha - 1, beta, gamma).

(2) Graphs with B' != B.  Then beta >= 1 and a is not adjacent to
    b = max B.  Now b ~ i, b ~ j, and the row of b has a 0 (at a) before a
    1 (at i), so it is non-decreasing by (M): N(b) = {q in S : q >= i} - b.
    Deleting b gives a graph of DU(S - b; i, j).  Conversely, adding b with
    this neighbourhood to H in DU(S - b; i, j) gives a graph of DU(S;i,j)
    in which a is not adjacent to b: here b is a twin of j, because p ~ b
    iff p > i iff p ~ j.  By Lemma 3 there are |DU(S - b; i, j)| such
    graphs, with parameters (alpha, beta - 1, gamma).

So, with m = alpha + beta + gamma, the induction hypothesis gives
|DU| = binomial(m-1, beta) + binomial(m-1, beta-1) = binomial(m, beta) if
beta >= 1, and |DU| = binomial(m-1, 0) = 1 = binomial(m, 0) if beta = 0.  []


4. THE CLASS DD

For an integer x put x^+ = max(x, 0).

PROPOSITION 2.
|DD(S;i,j)| = 2^( (beta - 1)^+ + (alpha + gamma - 1)^+ ).

Proof.  Induction on |S|.  Let G be in DD(S;i,j).

Case beta >= 2.  Every b in B is adjacent to i and not to j, so by (M)
N(b) is an initial segment of S - b that contains A + {i} and is contained
in A + {i} + B.  Let b1 = min B and b2 = max B.
 (1) Graphs with b1 ~ b2: N(b1) = {q in S : q < j} - b1.  Delete b1; it is
     a twin of i (p ~ b1 iff p < j iff p ~ i).
 (2) Graphs with b1 not adjacent to b2: N(b2) = A + {i}.  Delete b2; it is
     a twin of j (p ~ b2 iff p < i iff p ~ j).
In both cases Lemma 3 applies with F' the whole class DD of the smaller
vertex set, whose parameters are (alpha, beta - 1, gamma).  Hence
|DD| = 2 * 2^( (beta-2) + (alpha+gamma-1)^+ ), as claimed.

Case beta = 1.  For the unique b in B, N(b) = A + {i} is forced; b is a
twin of i.  Deleting it gives |DD| = |DD(S - b; i, j)|, with parameters
(alpha, 0, gamma), in agreement with the formula.

Case beta = 0, alpha >= 1 and gamma >= 1.  Now N(i) = N(j) = A, and i, j
are consecutive.  Let a = max A and c = min C.
 (1) Graphs with a ~ c.  The row of c has a 1 at a before a 0 at i, so by
     (M) c is adjacent to all of A and to nothing above i: N(c) = A.
     Delete c; it is a twin of j (p ~ c iff p < i iff p ~ j).  The
     parameters become (alpha, 0, gamma - 1).
 (2) Graphs with a not adjacent to c.  The row of a has a 1 at j before a
     0 at c, so by (M) N(a) = (A - a) + {i, j}.  Delete a; it is a twin of
     i (p ~ a iff p < i iff p ~ i, for p outside {i, j, a}).  The
     parameters become (alpha - 1, 0, gamma).
By induction each kind contains 2^(alpha+gamma-2) graphs, so
|DD| = 2^(alpha+gamma-1).

Case beta = 0 and alpha = 0 or gamma = 0.  By the Corollary we may assume
gamma = 0, so that S = A + {i, j} and N(i) = N(j) = A.  If alpha = 0 the
only graph is the empty graph on {i,j}.  If alpha = 1, the vertex of A is
adjacent to i and j and that is all: one graph.  Let alpha >= 2.  Every a
in A is adjacent to the two largest vertices i and j, so its row is
non-decreasing: N(a) is a final segment of S - a.  Let a1 = min A and
a2 = max A.
 (1) Graphs with a1 ~ a2: N(a2) contains the least vertex, so
     N(a2) = S - a2.  Delete a2; it is a twin of i (every p in A - a2 is
     adjacent to both).
 (2) Graphs with a1 not adjacent to a2: N(a1) = {i, j}.  Delete a1.  For
     the converse, add a new least vertex a1 with neighbourhood {i, j} to
     a graph H of DD(S - a1; i, j): the rows of i, j, a1 are monotone by
     inspection, and the row of any other p, which ends with 1, 1 and is
     therefore non-decreasing, gets a new first entry 0 and stays
     non-decreasing.
Lemma 3 gives |DD| = 2 |DD(alpha - 1, 0, 0)| = 2^(alpha-1).  []


5. SUMMATION

Proof of Theorem 2.  Let S = [n], n >= 3.  For i < j we have
beta = j - i - 1 and alpha + gamma = n - 2 - beta, and there are
n - 1 - beta pairs with a given beta (0 <= beta <= n-2).  By the Corollary
and Propositions 1 and 2, the number of flips (G, ij) is

    2 binomial(n-2, beta) + 2 * 2^( (beta-1)^+ + (n-3-beta)^+ ).

The exponent equals n - 3 if beta = 0 or beta = n - 2, and n - 4 otherwise.
So the number of flips at {i,j} is

    2 binomial(n-2, j-i-1) + 2^(n-2)   if j - i = 1 or j - i = n - 1,
    2 binomial(n-2, j-i-1) + 2^(n-3)   otherwise.

Summing the first term, with m = n - 2:

    sum_{beta} (m + 1 - beta) binomial(m, beta)
        = (m+1) 2^m - m 2^(m-1) = (m+2) 2^(m-1) = n 2^(n-3),

which contributes n 2^(n-2).  There are n pairs of the first kind and
n(n-1)/2 - n of the second, contributing

    n 2^(n-2) + (n(n-1)/2 - n) 2^(n-3) = n (n+1) 2^(n-4).

The total is n 2^(n-2) + n (n+1) 2^(n-4) = n (n+5) 2^(n-4).  []

With n = d + 3 this is (d+3)(d+8) 2^(d-1), which proves the Theorem.


6. REMARKS

Generating functions.  The Theorem is equivalent to each of

    sum_{d>=0} a(d) x^d = 4 (3 - 9x + 7x^2) / (1 - 2x)^3,
    sum_{d>=0} a(d) x^d / d! = 2 (x^2 + 6x + 6) exp(2x),
    a(d) = 6 a(d-1) - 12 a(d-2) + 8 a(d-3)  with  a(0..2) = 12, 36, 100.

The first is the empirical g.f. of C. Barker in the entry.

Formal verification.  Theorem 2, in the form "the number of pairs (G, e)
with e a non-edge of G and G, G + e consistent graphs on {0, ..., n-1}
equals n (n+5) 2^(n-4) for n >= 4, and 12 for n = 3", together with
Propositions 1 and 2, has been formalised and checked in Lean 4 with
Mathlib (about 2300 lines, no axioms beyond the three standard ones of
Lean/Mathlib).  The reduction of Section 1, that is facts (F1) and (F2), is
not part of the formalisation.  The sources are in ../ZonotopeFlips/; the
main statement is ZonoFlips.card_flipPairs_eq in ZonotopeFlips/Total.lean.

Sources of the cited facts.  Ziegler's paper [Z] was available only as a
scan and was not read directly: (F1) and (F2) are quoted in the form given
in [BW] and [C], and the reference to [Z, Proposition 7.1] is the one
recorded in OEIS A060602.
